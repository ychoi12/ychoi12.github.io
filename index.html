Thread 1

Fairness, explanation, and accountability

Auditing high-stakes models for disparate impact, and deciding what an affected person deserves to be told.

Carnegie Mellon · Ethics & AI · 2025 · team project

Responsible AI for personalized auto insurance

Question
Can an insurer personalize claim predictions and premiums while staying fair across gender, income, and age, and while explaining decisions to customers?
Method
Random Forest models for claim likelihood and claim amount. SHAP and DiCE counterfactuals for explanation. Fairlearn MetricFrame for intersectional audits, then ExponentiatedGradient (equalized odds) and BoundedGroupLoss for mitigation. A LLaMA 3.1 8B insurance assistant with RAG, red-teamed with the LlamaGuard S1–S13 taxonomy and ALERT prompts.
Finding
The intersectional audit found the worst-served group: low-income women under 30, at 69% accuracy against 84% for the best group. Mitigation cut the demographic parity gap by about 72% and kept over 90% of model performance.
0.11 → 0.03demographic parity gap
0.13 → 0.05equalized odds difference
$780 → $280income-based error gap

Where it fell shortThe LLM assistant refused clearly discriminatory requests, but it also blocked ordinary questions such as "How do DUIs affect premiums?" Over-blocking erodes trust and access too, so safety filters need their own fairness audit.

Harvard Extension · CSCI E-184 Data Science & AI · 2026 · team of six

When cost is mistaken for need

Question
How can health systems ethically use AI risk scores when the data used to predict patient need reflects historical inequities in access to care?
Method
Case analysis of the population-health algorithm studied by Obermeyer et al. (Science, 2019), compared with the UnitedHealth nH Predict litigation, organized around bias, transparency, and accountability.
Finding
The model excluded race but predicted cost rather than illness. Because patients with less access to care generate less spending, equally sick patients were scored as lower need. A tool labeled "advisory" also becomes binding once it is tied to operational targets.

TakeawayRace-neutral inputs do not produce race-neutral outcomes when the label itself is biased. Fairness rules have to be set before deployment, not established afterward in court.

Thread 2

Reliability of language AI

Building NLP and LLM systems, then testing whether their outputs deserve the confidence they project.

Harvard Extension · CSCI E-222 Large Language Models · 2026 · individual

Retrieval-augmented generation for open-domain question answering

Question
Does grounding a language model in retrieved passages make its answers more accurate, and can it recognize questions it cannot answer?
Method
DPR bi-encoder retriever, FAISS exact inner-product index over about 20,000 passages, and a Flan-T5 generator. Evaluated on 500 held-out SQuAD v2 questions, 258 of them deliberately unanswerable, with EM, F1, ROUGE-L, and Recall@K.
Finding
Retrieval raised exact match about 15× over the closed-book baseline, and accuracy climbed with each added passage up to K = 5.
0.004 → 0.062exact match, closed-book → RAG
0.116Recall@1, off-the-shelf retriever
258 / 258unanswerable questions still answered

Where it fell shortThe system never said "I don't know." Every unanswerable question received a confident answer. I proposed an answerability classifier on top of retrieval scores and fine-tuning the query encoder to close the domain gap.

Harvard Summer School · CSCI S-89 Deep Learning · 2026 · individual

Domain transfer for sentiment: airline complaints to ChatGPT discourse

Question
How well does a sentiment model trained on one domain transfer to another, and how far can we trust machine-generated labels?
Method
Bidirectional LSTM (1.3M parameters) trained on 14,427 human-labeled airline tweets, tested zero-shot on 201,162 ChatGPT tweets, then fine-tuned on 25,000 in-domain examples. A TF-IDF + logistic regression baseline ran on identical splits.
Finding
Zero-shot transfer fell below the majority-class baseline. Fine-tuning recovered most of the gap, and the linear baseline tracked the BiLSTM closely, so the gap came from the data, not the architecture.
0.353 → 0.731accuracy, zero-shot → fine-tuned
52.7%my labels agreed with the dataset's

Where it fell shortI hand-labeled 150 tweets blind as a sanity check. My judgments matched the dataset's machine-generated labels only 52.7% of the time. The fine-tuned model learned to reproduce the labeling tool faithfully, which is different from learning human sentiment.

Harvard Extension · CSCI E-89b Natural Language Processing · 2025 · individual

Fake news detection with deep learning

Question
What does it take to detect fake news reliably, and does model complexity pay off?
Method
Compared TF-IDF logistic regression, a dense network, LSTM, BiLSTM, and DistilBERT on 44,898 news articles, with error analysis, precision-recall curves, and training-cost comparison on free Colab hardware.
Finding
BiLSTM reached 98.86% accuracy, but logistic regression was only 0.43 points behind. On this dataset, fake news is detected mostly through vocabulary, which suggests the model learns source style rather than truthfulness.
98.86%BiLSTM accuracy
98.43%logistic regression accuracy

Where it fell shortDistilBERT collapsed to 52.29% with limited compute, and the LSTM peaked and then degraded. Near-perfect scores on a benchmark say little about how a detector holds up on new sources.

Thread 3

Data systems for service work

Applied projects with real clients, where the users were staff with little time and sensitive data.

Carnegie Mellon · MSBA Capstone · 2025 · team of four

Decision support for an early intervention provider

Problem
A provider of therapy services for young children ran 14 years of operations across five disconnected spreadsheets, so matching therapists to families was slow and error-prone.
My role
Cleaned and unified 30,000+ referral attempts, 66,000+ visit records, and 266 therapists under shared IDs. Automated preprocessing with Excel macros and Power Automate. Built dashboards for referral volume, caseload, therapist availability, and zip-code coverage.
Human side
After staff feedback, we moved the deliverable away from backend automation and toward clearer dashboards, and added the therapist comparison and zip-code filters staff asked for. We limited automation to non-sensitive fields for HIPAA compliance.

Where it fell shortA GPT pipeline for parsing free-text therapist availability missed recurring schedules and multi-location details. I documented these failure modes and designed a manual-verification fallback, so a person reviews the cases the model gets wrong.

University of Illinois Chicago · Supply Chain Capstone · 2023 · team of three

Sustainability drivers of operational performance

Problem
For the UIC Center for Supply Chain and Logistics: do environmental measures such as emissions, energy, and water relate to a company's operational performance?
Method
Built a standardized ESG and financial dataset from S&P Global and company sustainability reports across five industries, reconciling inconsistent units and field names. Used regression, correlation, and clustering in Excel and SPSS, with Tableau dashboards.
Finding
ESG and performance were related non-linearly. I delivered a repeatable methodology the sponsor could apply as new reports are published.
